# Production planning: finding the bottleneck before it delays the orders

*Draft for the Notion portfolio page. Synthetic data, real use case.*

**Tools:** Python · Excel · Power BI · Power Query · DAX · TMDL / PBIR (report as code)
**Repository:** github.com/lucasnunesf/production-planning-dashboard

---

## Context

AC Metais is a Brazilian metal fabrication company. Each production order goes through a routing of work centers: laser cutting, press brake bending, welding/boilermaking, finishing and painting, assembly and shipping. Planning (PCP) has to fit hundreds of operations into the hours each work center has.

## Problem

Delivery dates were promised based on the total workload of the shop. The total looked comfortable, but the hours were not evenly spread: one work center was overloaded, and every order that passed through it was late. There was no weekly view of load vs capacity per work center, nor a measure of how well the plan was followed.

## What I built

1. **Synthetic dataset (Python).** 754 orders and 3,225 operations over six months plus three weeks of plan ahead, with product families that need different hours per work center, delays that push the next operations, hour overruns, holidays and a maintenance stop, and a large tank order booked for November.
2. **Galaxy schema in Power BI.** Two fact tables (operations and daily capacity) sharing work center and calendar dimensions, plus an order dimension. Planned start is the active date; planned end is an inactive relationship used for adherence.
3. **11 DAX measures** for capacity (load, capacity, utilization, backlog, WIP, overloaded weeks) and execution (adherence, delay, hours deviation, late open operations).
4. **Two pages, each answering one business question:**
   - *Capacity vs load:* can the shop deliver the plan, and where is the bottleneck?
   - *Schedule adherence:* are we following the plan, and where do the hours go?
5. **Report as code.** Model, measures, 24 visuals and theme generated by Python in the Power BI Project format (TMDL + PBIR), with a reusable builder shared with my other dashboards, and validated against Microsoft's JSON schemas.

## Findings (synthetic scenario)

- The shop runs at 76% utilization overall, but welding averages 86%, goes above 100% in 7 weeks and reaches 138% in November.
- Welding also uses 19.5% more hours than planned, so the plan itself underestimates the bottleneck.
- 71.9% of operations finish by their planned end.

**Decisions it supports:** plan overtime or a temporary welder for November, correct the welding time standards, and check welding capacity before confirming large tank orders.

## What I learned

- Capacity and load live at different grains (day × work center vs operation), so they belong in separate fact tables joined by shared dimensions.
- A total utilization number hides bottlenecks; it has to be read by work center and week.
